Cardiovascular I10

Essential Hypertension

Clinical term: High Blood Pressure

Consistently elevated arterial blood pressure often called the 'silent killer' because it usually presents without noticeable symptoms until complications arise.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Often asymptomatic in early stages
  • Occasional morning headaches (occipital)
  • Mild dizziness or lightheadedness
Cardiovascular I25.1

Coronary Artery Disease & Angina

Clinical term: Ischemic Heart Disease

Narrowing or blockage of coronary arteries by atherosclerotic plaque, leading to reduced oxygen supply to heart muscle.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Substernal chest pressure, tightness, squeezing, or aching
  • Pain radiating to left shoulder, arm, jaw, neck, or back
  • Shortness of breath during mild exertion
Cardiovascular I50.9

Congestive Heart Failure

Clinical term: Cardiomyopathy / Heart Failure

A progressive clinical syndrome where the heart is unable to pump sufficient blood to meet the metabolic needs of peripheral tissues.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Progressive shortness of breath on exertion or lying flat (orthopnea)
  • Bilateral swelling in feet, ankles, and lower legs (peripheral edema)
  • Waking up at night gasping for breath (paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea)
Cardiovascular I48.91

Atrial Fibrillation & Arrhythmias

Clinical term: Cardiac Dysrhythmia

An irregular and often very rapid cardiac rhythm that can lead to blood clots, stroke, and heart failure.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Palpitations (fluttering, flip-flopping, or racing heart sensation)
  • Irregular pulse with erratic beats
  • Fatigue and generalized weakness
Cardiovascular I82.40

Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT)

Clinical term: Venous Thromboembolism

A blood clot that forms in a deep vein, usually in the lower extremity or pelvis, carrying a lethal risk of pulmonary embolism.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Unilateral swelling in one calf, thigh, or entire leg
  • Deep aching, cramping, or charley horse pain in the calf
  • Skin over the calf feels noticeably warmer to touch
Respiratory J45.9

Bronchial Asthma

Clinical term: Reactive Airway Disease

A chronic inflammatory disorder of the airways characterized by hyperresponsiveness, reversible airflow obstruction, and mucus hypersecretion.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • High-pitched expiratory wheezing
  • Episodes of breathlessness and air hunger
  • Chest tightness or feeling like an elastic band around ribs
Respiratory J18.9

Community-Acquired Pneumonia

Clinical term: Lower Respiratory Infection

An acute infection of the pulmonary parenchyma caused by bacteria, viruses, or fungi, causing the alveoli to fill with inflammatory exudate.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • High fever with shaking chills (rigors)
  • Productive cough with thick yellow, green, or rust-colored sputum
  • Pleuritic chest pain (sharp stab when inhaling deeply or coughing)
Respiratory J11.1

Influenza (The Flu)

Clinical term: Acute Viral Influenza

A highly contagious viral infection of the respiratory tract caused by Influenza virus A or B, featuring abrupt systemic toxicity.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Abrupt onset of high fever (100.4°F to 104°F / 38°C to 40°C)
  • Intense generalized body aches and myalgias
  • Severe exhaustion, weakness, and bedbound fatigue
Respiratory J20.9

Acute Bronchitis

Clinical term: Chest Cold / Tracheobronchitis

Self-limiting inflammation of the large bronchi, >90% caused by common respiratory viruses rather than bacteria.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Persistent cough that lasts 10 to 21 days
  • Clear, white, yellow, or greenish sputum production
  • Substernal chest soreness from repetitive coughing
Respiratory U07.1

COVID-19 (SARS-CoV-2)

Clinical term: Coronavirus Disease 2019

A systemic infection caused by the novel coronavirus SARS-CoV-2, affecting the upper/lower respiratory tract, vascular endothelium, and immune response.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Fever, chills, and rigors
  • Dry or productive cough
  • Sudden partial or total loss of taste or smell (Ageusia / Anosmia)
Respiratory J44.9

Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD)

Clinical term: Emphysema & Chronic Bronchitis

A progressive, life-threatening lung disease causing long-term breathing difficulty and persistent airflow limitation.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Chronic and progressive shortness of breath, initially on exertion
  • Daily chronic cough producing mucus/phlegm
  • Frequent respiratory infections in winter months
Neurological I63.9

Stroke & Transient Ischemic Attack (TIA)

Clinical term: Cerebrovascular Accident

A medical emergency caused by disrupted blood flow to the brain, either by arterial occlusion (ischemic) or vessel rupture (hemorrhagic).

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • F.A.S.T. signs: Facial drooping on one side
  • Arm weakness or numbness (inability to raise both arms)
  • Speech difficulty (slurred words or inability to speak/comprehend)
Neurological G43.9

Migraine Headache

Clinical term: Neurovascular Cephalea

A complex neurological condition featuring moderate-to-severe throbbing headache attacks, sensory hypersensitivity, and autonomic dysfunction.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Unilateral (one-sided) pulsating, throbbing head pain
  • Photophobia (extreme sensitivity to light) and phonophobia (sensitivity to sound)
  • Nausea and vomiting during peak pain
Neurological G44.2

Tension-Type Headache

Clinical term: Tension Cephalea

The most common primary headache disorder, presenting as a non-pulsatile, dull band of pressure around the forehead and temples.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Dull, aching, non-throbbing bilateral pressure or tightness ('vise-like band')
  • Discomfort extending from forehead to occiput and cervical spine
  • Tenderness in scalp, neck, and shoulder trapezius muscles
Neurological G51.0

Bell's Palsy

Clinical term: Idiopathic Facial Nerve Paralysis

Sudden, temporary weakness or paralysis of facial muscles on one side, resulting from acute dysfunction of Cranial Nerve VII (Facial Nerve).

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Rapid onset (over 24-72 hours) of total or partial paralysis on ONE side of the face
  • Inability to close the eye on the affected side or blink
  • Drooping mouth corner, drooling, and difficulty smiling or chewing
Gastrointestinal K21.9

Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD)

Clinical term: Acid Reflux / Reflux Esophagitis

A chronic digestive disease that occurs when stomach acid or bile flows back into the food pipe (esophagus), irritating the lining.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Pyrosis: burning chest pain (heartburn) behind breastbone, usually after meals
  • Acid regurgitation of sour or bitter fluid into mouth or throat
  • Worsening symptoms when lying flat, reclining, or bending over
Gastrointestinal K35.80

Acute Appendicitis

Clinical term: Inflammation of Vermiform Appendix

An acute, rapidly progressive inflammation and bacterial infection of the appendix that requires emergency surgical removal to prevent rupture.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Pain starting vaguely around the navel (periumbilical) then migrating to the LOWER RIGHT abdomen (McBurney's point)
  • Sharp, localized pain that intensifies rapidly over 12 to 24 hours
  • Pain dramatically worse with walking, jumping, coughing, or bumps during driving
Gastrointestinal K27.9

Peptic Ulcer Disease (PUD)

Clinical term: Gastric & Duodenal Ulceration

Open sores that develop on the inside mucosal lining of the stomach (gastric) or the upper portion of the small intestine (duodenal).

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Burning epigastric stomach pain between the breastbone and belly button
  • Pain often worse when the stomach is empty (duodenal ulcers improve with food, gastric worsen)
  • Pain that awakens patient during the middle of the night
Gastrointestinal K58.0

Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS)

Clinical term: Functional Gastrointestinal Disorder

A common disorder of brain-gut interaction characterized by recurrent abdominal pain linked to changes in bowel frequency or stool form.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Recurrent abdominal cramping or pain related to defecation
  • Altered stool frequency (diarrhea in IBS-D, constipation in IBS-C, or mixed in IBS-M)
  • Changes in stool appearance (lumpy/hard or loose/watery)
Gastrointestinal K80.00

Gallstones & Acute Cholecystitis

Clinical term: Biliary Colic & Cholecystitis

Hardened deposits of digestive fluid (cholesterol or bilirubin) in the gallbladder that can block the cystic duct, causing severe pain and inflammation.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Severe, steady, gripping pain in the RIGHT UPPER quadrant of the abdomen
  • Pain radiating to the right shoulder blade or interscapular back
  • Pain triggered 30 to 60 minutes after eating a fatty or greasy meal
Endocrine & Metabolic E11.9

Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus

Clinical term: Non-Insulin Dependent Diabetes

A chronic metabolic disorder characterized by persistent hyperglycemia resulting from insulin resistance combined with relative insulin secretory deficiency.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Polydipsia: unquenchable thirst despite drinking water
  • Polyuria: frequent urination, especially waking multiple times at night (nocturia)
  • Polyphagia: persistent hunger even after eating
Endocrine & Metabolic E03.9

Hypothyroidism (Underactive Thyroid)

Clinical term: Hashimoto's Thyroiditis / Thyroid Failure

A clinical state where the thyroid gland does not produce sufficient thyroid hormones (T3 and T4), slowing down the body's entire metabolic rate.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Persistent, debilitating fatigue and extreme sluggishness
  • Cold intolerance (feeling freezing when others are comfortable)
  • Unexplained modest weight gain (5-10 lbs, mostly fluid and salt)
Endocrine & Metabolic E05.0

Hyperthyroidism & Graves' Disease

Clinical term: Thyrotoxicosis

An overactive thyroid gland that produces and secretes excessive amounts of thyroid hormones, placing the body into a hypermetabolic state.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Rapid heart rate (resting tachycardia > 100 bpm) or palpitations
  • Unintentional weight loss despite normal or increased appetite
  • Heat intolerance and excessive perspiration
Musculoskeletal M19.90

Osteoarthritis

Clinical term: Degenerative Joint Disease

The most common form of arthritis, involving progressive biomechanical breakdown of articular cartilage, subchondral bone remodeling, and secondary synovitis.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Deep, aching joint pain that worsens after joint use and improves with rest
  • Morning joint stiffness lasting LESS than 30 minutes
  • Joint crepitus (grating, crunching, or cracking sensation on movement)
Musculoskeletal M54.30

Lumbar Radiculopathy (Sciatica)

Clinical term: Sciatic Nerve Compression

Pain radiating along the path of the sciatic nerve, typically branching from the lower back through the hip and buttock down one leg.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Sharp, burning, or electric-shock pain radiating down the buttock, posterior thigh, and calf
  • Pain typically felt in ONE leg (unilateral)
  • Pain aggravated by prolonged sitting, coughing, sneezing, or straining
Musculoskeletal M10.9

Gout & Hyperuricemia

Clinical term: Crystal-Induced Arthropathy

A painful form of inflammatory arthritis caused by the deposition of monosodium urate monohydrate crystals in synovial fluid and joint tissues.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Sudden, excruciating, explosive joint pain that awakens the patient in the middle of the night
  • Podagra: predominantly affects the first metatarsophalangeal (MTP) joint of the BIG TOE (> 50% of first flares)
  • Affected joint is intensely swollen, shiny red or purplish, and hot to touch
Musculoskeletal M79.7

Fibromyalgia

Clinical term: Central Sensitivity Syndrome

A chronic non-articular disorder characterized by widespread musculoskeletal pain, central pain amplification, debilitating fatigue, and sleep disturbances.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Widespread body pain present for at least 3 months, affecting both sides of the body, above and below the waist
  • Unrefreshing sleep: waking up feeling exhausted even after 8-10 hours in bed
  • Profound, relentless physical and mental fatigue
Dermatology L20.9

Atopic Dermatitis (Eczema)

Clinical term: Atopic Eczema

A chronic, relapsing, pruritic inflammatory skin disease characterized by epidermal barrier dysfunction and immune hyperreactivity.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Intense, persistent pruritus (itching) - often described as 'an itch that rashes'
  • Dry, erythematous (red), scaly patches of skin
  • Classic distribution in flexural creases (antecubital elbow creases, popliteal back-of-knee folds, neck, wrists)
Dermatology L40.0

Plaque Psoriasis

Clinical term: Psoriasis Vulgaris

A chronic systemic immune-mediated inflammatory skin condition characterized by sharply demarcated erythematous plaques covered with silvery micaceous scales.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Well-demarcated raised red plaques covered with coarse, flaky, silvery-white scales
  • Classic extensor surface distribution: outer elbows, front of knees, scalp, and lumbosacral back
  • Auspitz sign: pinpoint droplet bleeding when scales are gently lifted or scraped off
Dermatology B02.9

Shingles (Herpes Zoster)

Clinical term: Varicella-Zoster Virus Reactivation

A painful, blistering viral skin eruption caused by the reactivation of dormant varicella-zoster virus (VZV) in cranial or dorsal root sensory ganglia.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Prodrome: burning, tingling, stinging, or hyperesthesia in a localized skin band 2-4 days BEFORE any rash appears
  • Unilateral eruption of clustered erythematous papules that rapidly evolve into clear fluid-filled vesicles
  • Strict dermatomal distribution: stops sharply at the anatomical midline of the body (e.g. half-belt around torso)
Dermatology L03.90

Cellulitis

Clinical term: Acute Bacterial Dermis & Subcutaneous Infection

An acute, spreading bacterial infection of the deep dermis and subcutaneous tissue, most commonly affecting the lower legs.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Rapidly spreading area of warm, bright red, swollen skin with ill-defined borders
  • Skin feels hot and exquisitely tender to light pressure
  • Noticeable swelling and stretched, tight appearance of the affected limb
Urology N39.0

Urinary Tract Infection (UTI / Cystitis)

Clinical term: Acute Bacterial Cystitis

An infection in any part of the urinary system, most frequently the lower urinary tract (bladder and urethra) caused by ascending bacteria.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Dysuria: sharp, burning or stinging sensation while urinating
  • Urinary urgency: sudden, compelling urge to void that cannot be postponed
  • Urinary frequency: passing frequent, abnormally small volumes of urine
Urology N20.0

Kidney Stones (Nephrolithiasis)

Clinical term: Renal Calculi & Ureteral Colic

Hard mineral and salt deposits that form inside the renal calyces and cause excruciating pain when passing through the narrow ureter.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Severe, agonizing, colicky pain in the flank and back, directly below the ribs
  • Pain waves that radiate toward the lower abdomen, groin, and testicle/labia
  • Inability to find a comfortable position (patients pace, writhe, or constantly shift posture)
Infectious J02.0

Streptococcal Pharyngitis (Strep Throat)

Clinical term: Group A Streptococcal Tonsillopharyngitis

A bacterial infection of the throat and tonsils caused by Streptococcus pyogenes (Group A Strep) requiring antibiotics to prevent acute rheumatic fever.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Sudden, severe sore throat that causes agonizing pain when swallowing (odynophagia)
  • Fever typically >= 101°F (38.3°C)
  • Swollen, brightly red tonsils often coated with white or yellow patches of exudate
Mental Health F41.1

Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD)

Clinical term: Chronic Anxiety Neurosis

Persistent, excessive, and unrealistic worry about everyday life events, work, health, and finances that is difficult to control.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Excessive, uncontrollable anxiety and worry occurring more days than not for at least 6 months
  • Restlessness or feeling keyed up, on edge, or unable to relax
  • Muscle tension, particularly in the neck, shoulders, and upper back
Mental Health F32.9

Major Depressive Disorder (Depression)

Clinical term: Clinical Unipolar Depression

A common and serious mood disorder that negatively affects how you feel, think, and handle daily activities, lasting at least two consecutive weeks.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Persistent feeling of sadness, emptiness, hopelessness, or tearfulness
  • Anhedonia: markedly diminished interest or pleasure in almost all activities previously enjoyed
  • Significant unintentional weight loss/gain or marked changes in appetite
Mental Health F41.0

Panic Disorder & Panic Attacks

Clinical term: Episodic Paroxysmal Anxiety

An anxiety disorder characterized by recurrent, unexpected panic attacks and persistent worry about experiencing subsequent attacks.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Sudden surge of intense fear or intense discomfort that peaks within minutes
  • Palpitations, pounding heart, or accelerated heart rate
  • Sweating, trembling, or shaking
ENT & Eye J30.9

Allergic Rhinitis (Hay Fever)

Clinical term: Seasonal & Perennial Allergies

An IgE-mediated inflammatory condition of the nasal mucosa induced by airborne environmental allergen exposure.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Paroxysms of repetitive sneezing (often 5-10 times in a row)
  • Clear, watery rhinorrhea (runny nose) and nasal congestion
  • Intense itching of the nose, roof of the mouth, throat, and ears
ENT & Eye H66.90

Acute Otitis Media (Middle Ear Infection)

Clinical term: Tympanic Membrane Infection

An acute infection of the middle ear space behind the eardrum, characterized by rapid onset of pain and middle ear effusion.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Otalgia: sharp, throbbing, or deep aching ear pain
  • Temporary conductive hearing loss or muffled sound perception in affected ear
  • Feeling of fullness, pressure, or popping in the ear
ENT & Eye H10.9

Conjunctivitis (Pink Eye)

Clinical term: Inflammation of the Conjunctiva

Inflammation or infection of the transparent membrane (conjunctiva) that lines the eyelid and covers the white part of the eyeball.

Hallmark Symptoms:
  • Redness or pink discoloration in the sclera (white part of one or both eyes)
  • Discharge: watery clear discharge (viral/allergic) or thick yellow/green pus (bacterial)
  • Crusting of eyelids or eyelashes overnight, making it difficult to open eyes in the morning

Underlying Pathology Myofascial nociceptor sensitization in pericranial muscles, neck strain, and altered central pain processing without autonomic features.Cortical spreading depression and activation of the trigeminovascular system release calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP) and induce neurogenic inflammation.
Hallmark Symptoms
  • Dull, aching, non-throbbing bilateral pressure or tightness ('vise-like band')
  • Discomfort extending from forehead to occiput and cervical spine
  • Tenderness in scalp, neck, and shoulder trapezius muscles
  • Mild to moderate intensity that does not worsen with routine walking or stairs
  • Absence of nausea or vomiting
  • Unilateral (one-sided) pulsating, throbbing head pain
  • Photophobia (extreme sensitivity to light) and phonophobia (sensitivity to sound)
  • Nausea and vomiting during peak pain
  • Moderate to severe pain worsened by routine physical movement or bending
  • Attacks typically lasting 4 to 72 hours if untreated
Subtle / Atypical Signs
  • Difficulty concentrating due to persistent dull pressure
  • Mild light or sound sensitivity (never both simultaneously)
  • Visual aura: shimmering zigzag lights (fortification spectra), blind spots (scotoma) 20-60 min before headache
  • Sensory aura: numbness or tingling moving slowly across fingers or lips
  • Prodrome symptoms: excessive yawning, neck stiffness, food cravings 24h prior
Emergency Red Flags
  • ⚠️ Headache suddenly changes in quality, becoming sharp, unilateral, or throbbing
  • ⚠️ Accompanied by focal neurological symptoms (vision loss, weakness, speech slurring)
  • ⚠️ Awakens the patient from sleep in the middle of the night
  • ⚠️ Accompanied by unexplained weight loss, scalp tenderness on combing, or jaw claudication in older adults (Temporal Arteritis)
  • ⚠️ Headache reaching maximum explosive intensity within 60 seconds ('Thunderclap')
  • ⚠️ Headache accompanied by fever, stiff neck, and confusion (Meningitis sign)
  • ⚠️ Headache following head trauma or with new onset weakness/numbness
  • ⚠️ Progressive worsening daily headache in someone over age 50
Standard Diagnostic Workup
  • 🩺 Clinical evaluation and physical palpation of pericranial muscles
  • 🩺 Rule out cervical spine pathology and refractory vision refraction errors
  • 🩺 Clinical diagnosis based on International Headache Society (ICHD-3) criteria
  • 🩺 Brain MRI or CT only if red flags are present (abnormal neurological exam, onset after age 50, systemic symptoms)
Evidence-based Supportive Steps
  • 💡 Take ergonomic breaks every 30 minutes during desk work
  • 💡 Gentle neck and shoulder stretching and warm moist heat packs
  • 💡 Maintain consistent sleep and meal schedules
  • 💡 Judicious use of over-the-counter NSAIDs (Ibuprofen, Naproxen) no more than 2 days per week
  • 💡 Rest in a dark, quiet, cool room at the earliest sign of an attack
  • 💡 Apply cold compresses or ice packs to the forehead or base of skull
  • 💡 Stay hydrated and avoid known dietary triggers (aged cheeses, nitrates, alcohol)
  • 💡 Take abortive prescription triptans (Sumatriptan) or Gepants as soon as pain begins
Questions for the Doctor
  • ❓ Could my headaches be linked to teeth clenching (TMJ) or cervical neck posture?
  • ❓ How can I avoid medication overuse (rebound) headaches?
  • ❓ Would physical therapy or stress management techniques help?
  • ❓ Am I taking acute pain relievers too often (> 10 days/month), risking medication overuse headache?
  • ❓ Am I a candidate for daily preventive therapy (CGRP antagonists, beta-blockers, Topiramate)?
  • ❓ Should I keep a migraine headache trigger diary?

Critical Emergency Action Protocols

⚠️ Suspected Heart Attack

Call emergency services immediately. Sit down in a comfortable position with back supported. If not allergic and advised by emergency dispatch, chew one adult 325mg aspirin. Do NOT drive yourself to the hospital.

⚠️ Suspected Stroke (F.A.S.T.)

Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call 911 immediately. Note the exact time symptoms started. Do NOT give aspirin, food, or drink.

⚠️ Severe Anaphylaxis / Allergic Reaction

Administer an Epinephrine auto-injector (EpiPen) into the outer thigh immediately. Call emergency dispatch right away. Lie flat with legs elevated unless vomiting or struggling to breathe.

⚠️ Acute Mental Health Crisis

Call or text 988 (US/Canada) or your national crisis lifeline. Do not stay alone. Remove any immediate means of self-harm and connect with a trusted support person.
